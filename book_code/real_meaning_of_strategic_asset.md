# An asset that supports strategy versus an asset that participates in strategizing

Traditionally, a “strategic asset” means something that gives the enterprise an advantage over competitors. ERP, CRM, and BI once qualified because they improved coordination, visibility, and decision-making. But once nearly every competitor has comparable systems, the advantage becomes operational rather than strategic. 

BI is often described as strategic because it provides evidence for strategic decisions. But BI itself does not usually formulate strategy. It organizes measures, trends, exceptions, and comparisons so that people can reason about what to do.

An AI system, which includes a deterministic enterprise-wide knowledge graph, aspires to be a strategic formulator. It is not merely a repository of facts or another source of reports. It is intended to support the actual work of strategy by:

| Strategic capability                                 | What it contributes                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Connect evidence across domains**                  | Brings together observations from areas such as finance, sales, operations, customers, competitors, technology, and external events so strategy is not formulated from one departmental viewpoint. |
| **Expose relationships and trade-offs**              | Makes dependencies, conflicts, constraints, and competing objectives explicit—for example, how improving margin might affect growth, service levels, risk, or customer retention.                  |
| **Preserve enterprise concepts and prior reasoning** | Retains not only facts, but also definitions, assumptions, decisions, rationales, policies, and previous strategic thinking so the organization does not continually reason from scratch.          |
| **Identify analogous situations**                    | Connects the current situation to similar past events, cases, patterns, or external examples that may provide useful clues without assuming the situations are identical.                          |
| **Trace possible consequences**                      | Follows chains of relationships, dependencies, rules, probabilities, and processes to explore what might happen if a condition changes or a particular course of action is taken.                  |
| **Construct candidate courses of action**            | Helps combine goals, constraints, available capabilities, evidence, and possible consequences into alternative paths that humans can compare, challenge, and refine.                               |


That makes it's a strategic in a more literal sense: **it assists the enterprise in strategizing**.

Most **current** AI is powerful at synthesis, prediction, imitation, and recombination, but it is heavily conditioned by what has already been written, observed, or rewarded. It tends to produce plausible continuations of existing patterns. That is often very useful tactically, but strategy requires more than extending the present. It requires choosing a future state, recognizing that current assumptions may no longer hold, evaluating competing paths, and sometimes inventing a position that does not yet exist.

A useful way to frame the contrast is:

| Technology | Primary contribution                                                                    |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ERP        | Coordinates enterprise operations                                                       |
| CRM        | Organizes customer relationships                                                        |
| BI         | Provides evidence for human decision-making                                             |
| Current AI | Synthesizes and extrapolates from existing patterns                                     |
| Your KG    | Organizes the enterprise’s knowledge so humans and AI can reason about possible futures |

Most so-called strategic systems support people who strategize. The knowledge graph described here is intended to become part of the strategizing process itself.

To clarify, I'm not claiming that the KG autonomously **creates** strategy <i>at this time</i>. The strength of the KG itself is as a **consultative reasoning environment** in which enterprise knowledge, evidence, rules, models, and alternatives can be brought together. That is a stronger and more credible claim than saying the graph itself is intelligent.
